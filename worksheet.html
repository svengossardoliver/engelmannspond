4 · Plan your time

Every count of bubbles lasts as long as you set it to, usually 60 s.

If your teacher has set manual counting, each count is a real minute of watching and tapping. The lab clock cannot speed it up.

Number of conditions (e.g. wavelengths)SprigsCounts per sprig per conditionMinutes per countTotal minutes of counting

Total = conditions × sprigs × counts × minutes per count. Add time for setting up and for the sprig to settle (about 3 minutes after each change).

Counting by hand, a full action spectrum is about 25 minutes of counting with one tube and about 45 minutes with three tubes, one tube at a time. Your counts are saved in the browser, so finish on the same device and browser you started on.

Things students often miss

  • Setting the light with the PAR meter, not the lux meter or the brightness dial.
  • Waiting for the sprig to settle after every change.
  • Switching the room lights off.
  • Refreshing the NaHCO₃ during a long run.
  • More than one sprig: repeated counts of one sprig are not independent replicates.
  • The LED is not monochromatic: Δλ comes from the spectrometer.

Before choosing manual counting, plan the time. Each count is a real minute. A full action spectrum is about 25 minutes of counting with one tube and about 45 minutes with three, plus set-up. Plan a double lesson or split it across lessons; counts stay on the same device and browser.
